Pan-malignant AAV enhancer candidates — GBM atlas v3 (2026-10-07)

Source atlas: 8 cohorts, 51 patients (45 contributing ≥10 cells to pan-malignant pool), 1,523,668 nuclei total, 575,971 pan-malignant cells after CNV + label join. Pool rule: malignant_cnv == 1 OR cell_type == malignant_unresolved.

What changed from v2

  1. Fixed CNV missing for fragments-mode cohorts — v2 silently defaulted malignant_cnv=0 for tcga_scatac and gse276177 because src/flatten_cohort.py skipped the CNV calling path. Patched to call _parallel_chr7_10_ratio in-process; recovered 12 patients and 127,045 malignant cells for the pool. Candidate pool size up accordingly.
  2. Patient denominator updated 33 → 45 for n_patients_detected normalization in aav_score.
  3. Cohort QC caveats incorporated — see Methods / Caveats section below. Short version: hra004942 is low-cell-per-patient (20-250 malignant/pt), gse165037 is sci-ATAC (not 10x), gse276177 is really Sojka et al. 2025 (slug misnomer), guilhamon confirmed hg38 (2,332 malignant cells across 4 pts match CATLAS peaks).
Important caveat (preserved from v2). The v1 top-15 was dominated by promoter-proximal peaks (PRKCSH, FEM1A, UBAP2, MCM3, VCP, STAT2…). These are housekeeping promoters, open in every proliferating cell — exactly what you DON'T want for AAV cell-type-selective targeting. v2+ filters them out so the shortlist reflects distal, malignant-selective enhancer candidates.

Daigle Z-score — Z = (strengthpan-mal − meanother) / sdother, where "other" = non-malignant / non-unassigned cell types (astrocyte, oligo, OPC, neuron, GABA_neuron, microglia, TAM, endothelial, T_cell). Z ≥ 2 is the standard Armamentarium threshold for calling a peak "cell-type specific." Columns in the output: z_daigle, passes_daigle_z2, nonmal_mean, nonmal_sd.

Caveats / limits of this analysis

  • chr7 excluded on principle. chr7+ gain is universal in GBM, so chr7 peaks have inflated strength from CNV, not cell-type selectivity. Even if a chr7 peak is in a biologically perfect spot, re-ranking against a CNV-matched reference is required before trusting it.
  • Selectivity reference is coarse. We compare malignant strength to the max strength across non-malignant cell types (TAM, neuron, OPC, microglia, etc.). A peak that's very open in e.g. neurons will score poorly on selectivity even if it's clearly tumor-driving in a non-neuron context. Future iteration: compare malignant vs each specific non-malignant type.
  • Promoter cutoff is 2 kb. Some bona fide enhancers sit within 2 kb of a TSS. If a candidate looks great on all other metrics but gets excluded by the distal filter, pull it from pan_malignant_top100_all_v3.csv and inspect manually.

Cohort-level methods caveats (new in v3)

Per the methods comparison report (reports/methods_comparison.md). These impact how much weight to put on cross-cohort replication for a given peak:

  • hra004942 (gbm_tme_atlas) — only 20-250 malignant cells per patient (vs 10K-80K in gbm_space). Platform may be low-throughput snATAC or a different chemistry than 10x. A peak replicated here contributes less information than one replicated in a 10K-cell patient. Peaks relying heavily on this cohort for replication should be viewed cautiously.
  • gse165037 — sci-ATAC (combinatorial indexing, Sinnamon et al.-style), not 10x droplet. Peaks per cell much shallower; co-opening patterns can diverge from droplet. Treat as orthogonal technology; agreement here is extra-strong evidence.
  • gse276177 (slug "khan_astro") — actually Sojka et al. 2025 Nature Cell Biol. Rename TODO; data content and analysis are correct. Three IDH-WT GBM patients, 10x snATAC.
  • guilhamon — pan-malignant pool matches CATLAS peaks (2,332 cells, 10-25K accessible peaks per patient) which confirms hg38 build. 4 patients, 500-700 malignant cells each.
  • tcga_scatac (sundaram_gbm) — recovered in v3 after CNV fix. 9 patients, 53,632 malignant cells. CATLAS peak coverage is 540K+ per patient (near-saturated), unusually deep.
